Our Story

From One Mother's Struggle in Kiboga

Sarang Adonai means "Shelter of the Lord." This is the story of how a single mother's sacrifice became a school, and a school became a movement to help vulnerable families across a nation.

Mama Rose
Nalongo
Chapter One

Mama Rose Nalongo

Mama Rose Nalongo was born in Kampala and raised eight children as a single mother. When the city could no longer feed her family, she made a decision that would change generations: she travelled to Kiboga to dig the farm.

She dug. She planted. She sold. And with every shilling she earned from that soil, she put her children through school. She had no capital, no connections, and no safety net — only an unshakeable belief that education was the one thing poverty could not take away.

R O S E  +  T E
Chapter Two

The Meaning of ROSETE

The name ROSETE was not chosen at random. It is a fusion of two women who shaped a family:

ROSE — for Mama Rose, the mother who dug the farm.

TE — for the grandmother, the quiet pillar behind her.

Every child who walks through the gates of Rosete Junior School walks into a name that carries two generations of sacrifice.

The Founder
Last-Born Twin
Chapter Three

The Last-Born Twin

The founder was the last-born twin among Mama Rose's eight children. There was never enough money — but there was talent. A gifted athlete, he paid his own school fees through sport.

Not satisfied with only surviving, he began teaching in different schools, using his small earnings to support his siblings' education. He discovered something that would define his life: teaching multiplies hope.

One Rented Room
Kayunga-Wakiso
Chapter Four

From One Rented Room to 300+ Pupils

In Kayunga-Wakiso, the founder rented a single room. That room became Rosete Junior School — the first classroom, the first blackboard, the first pupils.

Today, Rosete Junior School serves 300+ pupils from Nursery to P7, with a feeding program and low fees. And through ApShule, those same lessons now travel far beyond Kayunga-Wakiso to children across Uganda and beyond.

Sarang Adonai
Shelter of the Lord
Chapter Five

Sarang Adonai — Shelter of the Lord

The foundation that holds Rosete School and ApShule together is named Sarang Adonai — Shelter of the Lord.

It is a declaration that this work was never built by one person. It was sheltered, protected and multiplied by God, through the hands of a mother, a grandmother, a founder, and now a community of supporters around the world.

Beyond the
Classroom
Chapter Six

Helping Needy & Vulnerable Families Across Uganda

While our foundation began with education, our mission quickly expanded. We realized that a child cannot thrive at school if their family is struggling to survive at home.

Today, Sarang Adonai Rosete Foundation reaches out to needy and vulnerable families across the country. We provide essential relief — food, shelter support, and basic needs — to those who have nowhere else to turn. We believe in being a true Shelter of the Lord for the most desperate among us.

What Is ApShule?

A Classroom That Fits in Your Pocket

ApShule is our digital classroom platform. Every lesson is recorded by real Rosete teachers, uploaded to appshule.com, and made available to children everywhere.

🕐

Available 24/7

Learning doesn't stop when school closes. Children can watch lessons any hour, any day.

💵

10,000 UGX per Term

A fraction of private tuition. One small termly fee unlocks the whole library.

⬇️

Offline Download

No data? No problem. Lessons can be downloaded and watched completely offline.

How It Works

From Kayunga-Wakiso to Any Home in Four Steps

1

Record

Rosete teachers record lessons in Kayunga-Wakiso, using the same syllabus they teach in class.

2

Upload

Lessons are uploaded to appshule.com by our ApShule Tech Lead.

3

Watch

The child logs in and watches the lesson — at home, at a neighbour's, or at school.

4

Learn Offline

Downloaded lessons play without internet, so learning continues even where data is scarce.
